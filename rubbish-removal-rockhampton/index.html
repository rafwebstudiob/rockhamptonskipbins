Rubbish Removal Rockhampton: Skip Bins vs Pick-up

Your options for rubbish removal in Rockhampton

In short: For a big clean-up, a skip bin is usually the simplest way to remove household or garden waste. For a load or two, the Lakes Creek Road facility may be enough, and some recyclables and scrap metal are free to drop off.

Most Rockhampton households have two ways to get rid of more rubbish than fits in their bins:

  1. A skip bin, delivered to your driveway and taken away when you're done.
  2. Taking it to the tip yourself, at Rockhampton Regional Council's Lakes Creek Road Waste Management Facility or a smaller council waste facility.

Which one makes sense depends on how much you have, what it is, and whether you have a trailer and the time to do the runs.

Option 1: a skip bin

In short: A skip suits jobs with a lot of mixed rubbish, heavy waste or many trailer loads of garden waste, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one go.

A skip sits on your driveway (or on the street with council approval) for an agreed hire period. You can fill it over a few days, and you don't need a trailer, a ute or anyone to lift heavy items. We couldn't find a regular council kerbside bulky waste pick-up for Rockhampton, so for furniture, garden waste, soil, bricks or renovation waste, a skip saves the tip runs, as long as you choose the right bin and tell the operator. They can't take asbestos, gas bottles, batteries, tyres or chemicals in a normal load. See what can go in a skip and the size guide.

For a deceased estate, a house move or a big shed or garage job, see our clean-outs guide.

Option 2: the Lakes Creek Road tip

In short: If you have a trailer and only a load or two, you can take rubbish to council's Lakes Creek Road facility yourself. Some items are free for domestic customers. General waste and green waste are charged.

Rockhampton Regional Council's Lakes Creek Road Waste Management Facility lists its hours as 7am to 5pm, Monday to Sunday, closed on Christmas Day. It has a waste transfer station, a resource recovery area, a Community Recycling Centre and a reuse shop. Council's smaller waste facilities, including Gracemere and Mount Morgan, accept domestic waste only.

  • Free for domestic customers at the Community Recycling Centre: household recycling, e-waste, reusable household items (at staff discretion), cooking and motor oil, car batteries, paint, small empty gas bottles, fire extinguishers and small scrap metal.
  • Free over the weighbridge: large scrap metal, large whitegoods such as stoves and washing machines (not fridges, freezers or air conditioners), and less than 1 tonne of clean soil for domestic customers.
  • Charged: general household waste, green waste, fridges and air conditioners, mattresses, tyres, timber, construction and demolition waste, and asbestos (larger asbestos loads must be pre-booked).

Does Rockhampton have a kerbside bulky waste pick-up?

In short: We couldn't find a regular one. Council's kerbside service covers your bins, and bulky items go to its waste facilities.

Rockhampton Regional Council's bins and collections information covers weekly general waste and fortnightly recycling, and we couldn't find a regular kerbside bulky waste or hard rubbish collection for residents. Items that won't fit in your bin can be taken to Lakes Creek Road or another council waste facility. Good-condition items can go to the reuse shop.

What about my kerbside bins?

In short: Your bins are for everyday rubbish and recycling, not clean-ups.

In council's declared collection areas, the red-lid general waste bin is collected weekly and the yellow-lid recycling bin fortnightly. A green bin isn't part of the standard service, although council has trialled organics collections in some areas.

So which is easier?

In short: Pick a skip for big, mixed or heavy jobs. Use Lakes Creek Road for a load or two, especially free items like scrap metal and recycling.

  • A skip bin usually makes sense for a full clean-out, a renovation, a big garden or shed job, heavy waste, or when you don't have a trailer or the time for several tip runs.
  • The Lakes Creek Road facility suits one or two trailer loads, and scrap metal, large whitegoods and recycling are free for domestic customers.
  • The reuse shop suits household items that are still in good condition.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to get rid of a lot of rubbish in Rockhampton?

It depends on how much you have and what it is. For one or two loads, a trailer run to the Lakes Creek Road Waste Management Facility may be enough. For a big clean-out, heavy waste or many loads, a skip bin is often the easier option, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one trip.

When is the Rockhampton tip open?

Rockhampton Regional Council lists its Lakes Creek Road Waste Management Facility as open 7am to 5pm, Monday to Sunday, closed on Christmas Day.

What can I drop off free at the Rockhampton tip?

For domestic customers, council lists free drop-off for household recycling, e-waste, reusable household items, oils, car batteries, paint, small gas bottles, fire extinguishers and scrap metal, plus large whitegoods (not fridges or air conditioners) and under 1 tonne of clean soil. General and green waste are charged.

Does Rockhampton Regional Council pick up bulky rubbish?

We couldn't find a regular kerbside bulky waste collection for Rockhampton residents. Bulky items can be taken to the Lakes Creek Road facility or another council waste facility.

Is there a green waste bin in Rockhampton?

A green bin isn't part of council's standard kerbside service. Green waste can be taken to a council waste facility, where it's charged, or put in a green waste skip.

Can a skip bin take garden waste and building rubbish?

Usually, yes, which is one reason people choose a skip. Heavy waste such as soil, concrete and bricks normally needs a smaller bin with a weight limit, and asbestos can never go in a general skip. Tell the operator what you have when you request a quote.

Do I need a permit for a skip bin on the street in Rockhampton?

A skip wholly on your own property generally doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or verge usually needs approval from Rockhampton Regional Council. See our council skip permits guide for details.
